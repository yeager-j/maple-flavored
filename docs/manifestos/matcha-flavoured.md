# What I want from Matcha Flavoured, and what I don't

Jackson's opinions from playing Matcha Flavoured, which shape Maple Flavored. They're a summary of intent rather than a quote, and they explain why the design leans the way it does. Where a resolved ticket on the design map differs, the ticket wins.

## The starting point

Maple Flavored isn't a port of Matcha. I like Matcha's direction, but we start from vanilla and include features on purpose, rather than copying Matcha and removing what we don't like. There's no point writing down "don't rename gunpowder". It should just never come up.

I'm a technical player, and I enjoy building farms. Matcha is designed for players who don't, and that's the biggest difference between us.

## What I love

- **Crystal Hearts and losing a heart on death.** Losing a heart instead of your items makes death punishing without making you want to quit. This is the mechanic most worth taking, with credit.
- **A Heart Floor that falls as you progress.** Early deaths can't ruin a save.
- **Alloys.** Netherite stops being strictly the best material. We should have an alloy system of our own that's distinct from Matcha's.
- **Earning villagers.** Finding a village early skips a huge amount of progression, and an iron farm becomes the first thing you build. Working to get villagers fixes that. Iron farms should still be possible, but a day 1 iron farm is a little overpowered.
- **Structures.** Matcha's changes and additions are good, and the Abbey is really cool.
- **Mob changes.** Fast zombies are terrifying. Lower health on skeletons and baby zombies is much needed.
- **Zephyr.** It may be the best enchantment ever.

## What I don't

- **Discouraging farms.** Farm building shouldn't be punished or made pointless.
- **Changes that only serve lore.** Did we need Gunpowder to become Sulfur, Netherrack to become Hellstone, or a replacement for Ender Pearls? Matcha establishing its own lore is fine, but it isn't what this mod is for.
- **Early shulker boxes.** Having them almost immediately is nice, but it makes End Cities mostly pointless to explore. Their loot is better, but there's no reason to collect it: I have 8 stacks of iron blocks and a stack of diamond blocks doing nothing. In vanilla, that iron would go into redstone contraptions or be traded for emeralds. Vanilla's bundles are fine early on, and Traveler's Backpack is great if you want more storage early.
- **Matcha's healing and food.** The early game is a slog until a Cook villager sells you incredible food for cheap, and then food becomes irrelevant. That isn't far from vanilla's arc, but if I had to choose, I'd take vanilla's food and hunger. I think we can do better than both.
- **Enchanting and brewing.** Matcha's versions are modestly better than vanilla's, but still not great.
- **Early-game progression that doesn't last.** The mud kiln, oven and blast furnace aren't very impactful in the long run. Making a campfire your first source of heat is cool, but an hour into a save you've forgotten about it.
- **Permanent world changes.** Beating the Ender Dragon removing surface mob spawning is interesting, but permanent changes aren't in the spirit of an infinite sandbox.
- **Sidelining XP.** It has basically been removed as a mechanic, and it's now only used for Mending.

## Why Breath of the Wild's cooking doesn't transfer directly

Matcha's cooking was clearly inspired by Breath of the Wild, which has a great cooking mechanic. But Minecraft is a different game.

- **Damage works the other way round.** Breath of the Wild is an action game where you can dodge and block, and most attacks hit hard but rarely. In Minecraft you take damage often, from falls and especially ranged mobs, and the shield is the only good way to avoid it.
- **Food heals too little.** In Breath of the Wild, food restores a large share of your health, especially early on with 3 hearts. Most of Matcha's early food restores about 2 hearts, and the best I found, a roasted potato, restores 2.5.
- **Food heals too slowly.** Matcha heals through ticks of Regeneration III, so it takes time. In Breath of the Wild it's instant from the pause menu.
- **Food effects encroach on brewing.** Breath of the Wild has elixirs too, but I rarely used them because food gave me what I needed.

For Minecraft, vanilla handles food best so far.
